# Transfers into the scheme from members' previous pensions

Each of the three individual members brought a previous workplace pension into the scheme by
cash transfer, arranged through the administrator without financial advice. The individual
transfer values are member benefit figures and are **not repeated here**; they are in the Raw
files. What is recorded is who transferred what plan, when, and the aggregate, because the
aggregate is the best available estimate of the scheme's size for the loanback's 50 percent test.

**Aggregate.** The three Aviva transfer values quoted in the administrator's letters [^2] [^3]
[^4] sum to 99,956.22. Adding the approximate Nest value written on the application form [^7]
gives 107,196.27. These are the ceding providers' quotations at the letter dates, not receipts;
**no scheme bank statement is on file to show what arrived or when** `(unverified)`. Gmail adds
timing: the first director's transfer was received in June 2023 [^10]; the second director's was
still being safeguarded in July 2023 [^11]; the third member's Aviva transfer was still not

directors' transfers can have been in the scheme**. Their two quoted values sum to 83,405.00
(99,956.22 less the third member's 2024 quotation), and 41,500 is 49.8 percent of that.

## Details

The pattern is the same each time: the member signs an Empowered Pensions "Authority Form -
Information Only Request" so the administrator can obtain the plan details; the ceding provider
issues a transfer illustration and a questionnaire designed to detect pension scams; the
administrator writes to the member confirming that no advice was sought or given and the
member signs a "client agreement" to the same effect; the member signs the provider's transfer
form. The questionnaires ask whether a loan to the sponsoring employer is intended; the 2022 and
2023 answers for M Gaudiesius record the intended investment as commercial property and no loan
[^1]. The scheme's first known investment was in fact a loanback, made in April 2025; see
[Loanback to Fishbone Commercial Properties Ltd](../Assets/loanback-fishbone-commercial-properties.md).

The 2022 questionnaire names the trustees as M Gaudiesius, A Prutkovas and Empowered Trustees
Ltd (12291059) [^1] p.3; the 2023 one describes Empowered Pensions Ltd as the independent
corporate trustee, which is the administrator's name rather than the trustee company's
`(legacy folder)`. The third member's 2025 questionnaire relies on employment by Fishbone
Properties Ltd for the employment link, while the employer letters say Fishbone Drylining Ltd is
the sponsoring company; see [Fishbone Properties Ltd](../Employers/fishbone-properties-ltd.md).

For the scheme's size: the 41,500 loanback is about 39 percent of the Aviva aggregate alone and
about 41 percent of the aggregate including Nest, before any contributions or growth. ~~So on
these figures the 50 percent limit would have been met at April 2025.~~ Corrected the same day
from Gmail: the third member's transfers were not in the scheme by April 2025, so the relevant
base is the two directors' quoted values, 83,405.00, against which the loan is about 49.8
percent. Whether the scheme's actual value at 16/04/2025 (transfers received, plus any
contributions and interest, less fees) exceeded 83,000 decides the test `(unverified: values,
not receipts)`. See [Loanback](../Assets/loanback-fishbone-commercial-properties.md).

Aviva's letter of 18/07/2025 also says the member told Aviva that "Barnett Waddingham" were the
administrator charging the fees [^12]; the scheme's administrator is Empowered Pensions, so
either the member's answer or Aviva's letter is wrong `(unverified)`.
